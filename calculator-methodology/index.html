Calculator Methodology

The formulas, conventions and checks behind every TrueWealthMetrics calculator, so you can see exactly how each result is produced.

Last updated 11 November 2026

Principles

  • One shared engine. Every calculator uses the same calculation library, so identical inputs give identical results across the site. For example, the compound interest, investment, fee and ROI calculators all agree.
  • Calculated in your browser. Inputs are never sent to a server.
  • No hidden rounding. Values are calculated at full precision and rounded only for display. Real statements can differ by a few pence or cents because lenders and providers round each period.
  • Assumptions alongside results. Every results panel lists the assumptions used, and every calculator page ends with its formula and limitations.

Conventions used across the site

Loans, mortgages and cards
Monthly interest rate = annual rate ÷ 12. Payments are made at the end of each month. Level payment: M = P × i ÷ (1 − (1 + i)^−n).
Savings and investments
The annual rate is a nominal rate compounded k times a year (monthly by default). Each month the balance grows by (1 + r/k)^(k/12). Monthly contributions are added at the end of each month unless you choose otherwise.
Fees
An annual percentage fee is charged monthly as one-twelfth of the annual rate on the balance after growth.
Inflation
Today’s-money value = future value ÷ (1 + inflation)^years.
Annualised return
CAGR for a single amount: (final ÷ start)^(1/years) − 1. When money is added over time, the money-weighted return (IRR) is used instead, because CAGR would overstate or understate the true rate.
Safety limits
Month-by-month schedules stop after 100 years and say so, rather than running indefinitely.

Calculator-by-calculator notes

Each calculator page has a How this calculator works section with its exact formula, conventions, limitations and jurisdiction:

  • Budget planner: monthly totals, needs/wants/savings shares compared with the 50/30/20 guide, emergency fund = essential costs × months.
  • Savings goal: time to goal (simulated) and required monthly saving (closed form).
  • Loan repayment and mortgage: standard amortisation with optional overpayments.
  • Credit card payoff: fixed or percentage-of-balance payments, with a warning when the payment does not cover the interest.
  • Snowball vs avalanche: both strategies simulated side by side, with freed-up minimums rolled over.
  • Compound interest, investment, fee impact and ROI: shared growth engine.
  • Retirement planning: projected pot, target pot from a withdrawal rate, and the monthly saving needed.

How calculators are tested

Automated tests run before any calculator change is published. They compare results with values calculated independently of the site’s own code:

  • published textbook values (for example, a 200,000 loan at 6% over 30 years costs 1,199.10 a month, and 100 a month for 10 years at 6% compounded monthly grows to 16,387.93);
  • closed-form formulas for lump sums, annuities, payoff months and total interest;
  • round-trip checks (for example, the monthly saving the retirement calculator says you need really does reach the target pot);
  • edge cases: 0% interest, negative returns, payments that never clear a debt, goals already reached or unreachable, invalid or blank inputs, and very large values.

Limitations that apply to every calculator

  • Rates, returns and inflation are held constant. Real ones change, and investment returns can be negative.
  • Tax, tax relief, benefits and country-specific product rules are not modelled unless a calculator explicitly says so.
  • Results are estimates for education and planning. They are not guarantees or personal advice.

Change log

  • 11 November 2026: all calculators moved to one shared, tested calculation engine. The ROI calculator now uses the selected compounding frequency, supports losses, and reports a true annualised return (CAGR or money-weighted IRR) instead of an approximation. Compound, investment, fee and retirement calculators now use the same compounding and contribution-timing rules, so their results match. Invalid inputs now clear the results and explain what to fix, instead of leaving the previous result on screen. The savings goal rate is now labelled as an annual rate compounded monthly, matching the maths. The mortgage calculator labels property tax and HOA/service charges as optional, country-dependent costs.
